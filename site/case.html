How the fingerprint works

One string, defined once, hashed in three places

The approved deal is written as one line, in this exact order, joined with |: TT1 | case number | retailer | invoice number | invoice date | bill value | discount % | discount amount | approved by | approved on. Numbers have no separators, dates are YYYY-MM-DD, text is UTF-8. That line is run through SHA-256.

  • ServiceNow hashed it with GlideDigest at the moment of approval, and stored the seal on the record.
  • Ten Today's engine rebuilds the line from the live record and hashes it with its own SHA-256 code (no crypto library).
  • Your browser rebuilds the line again and hashes it with Web Crypto.

If all three agree, the approved terms are exactly what was approved. If anyone changed the amount, the retailer or the approver afterwards, the line changes, the hash changes, and check 7 fails. This is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: it can't stop an edit, it makes every edit visible.
