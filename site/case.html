The story · 5 beats, in the order the record logged them

  1. ₹64,000 given back. Neha in sales asks to give Meera Trading 8% back on an ₹8 lakh March bill. To pay for itself, Meera has to buy 18.5% more than usual.
  2. Approved by the right person. Neha tries to approve it herself and is refused. Vikram in finance approves it, at exactly his 8% limit. The approved terms are sealed.
  3. Rejected on the GST portal. The credit note goes out; Meera rejects it: "slab not met". ₹11,520 of tax relief is now at risk.
  4. Caught, owned, on a clock. ServiceNow opens an incident for Asha in Receivables, with a 48-hour SLA tracked by the platform, and blocks new discounts to Meera. The cash, ₹7,36,000, arrives and matches to the rupee.
  5. Settled, and judged. Vikram settles it as a financial credit note: ₹11,520 of GST given up, stored on the case as a settlement, not hidden in a note. At period close C1 gives its verdict: Meera bought 0% more against 18.5% needed, so the ₹64,000 bought no sales. That opens an incident for Vikram.

Every Rupee · beat 4, across all ten

Did the right money arrive?

Meera's ₹7,36,000 matched to the rupee. Across the five retailers who have paid, one did not: Royal Foods, ₹63,000 short. Every Rupee sent that gap to ServiceNow, where it became an incident for Asha.

        My decisions · five calls I made, and what each one cost

        Five calls I made, and what each one cost

        • Finance approves, up to 8%. Post-sale money is finance's call, so Vikram approves, not a sales manager. The limit lives in a ServiceNow decision table finance can change without a developer. Cost: one more hand-off for sales.
        • Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. The seal can't stop an edit; it makes every edit visible. I won't claim more than it does.
        • No back-dated history. Every entry carries the time it really happened, marked "Sample walkthrough". The demo would look smoother with a March-to-November timeline; it would also be fiction.
        • Pivoted on the rule change. Ten Today began as a sales rep's top-ten to-do list. When the GST rules changed on 1 Nov 2026, the real risk moved to money given back after the sale, so the product moved with it.
        • Native first, code where it earns its place. Approvals, SLAs, incidents, UI policies and tests use ServiceNow's own features. Custom code is kept for the seal and the endpoints other systems call.

        How the fingerprint works

        One string, defined once, hashed in three places

        The approved deal is written as one line, in this exact order, joined with |: TT1 | case number | retailer | invoice number | invoice date | bill value | discount % | discount amount | approved by | approved on. Numbers have no separators, dates are YYYY-MM-DD, text is UTF-8. That line is run through SHA-256.

        • ServiceNow hashed it with GlideDigest at the moment of approval, and stored the seal on the record.
        • Ten Today's engine rebuilds the line from the live record and hashes it with its own SHA-256 code (no crypto library).
        • Your browser rebuilds the line again and hashes it with Web Crypto.

        If all three agree, the approved terms are exactly what was approved. If anyone changed the amount, the retailer or the approver afterwards, the line changes, the hash changes, and check 7 fails. This is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: it can't stop an edit, it makes every edit visible.
