    The decisions I'd defend

    What I left out, and why

    Each of these would look impressive in a demo and make the product worse. Deciding what not to build is half the job.

    1. No AI deciding the discount. The rules decide and the numbers are shown; an approver can follow every one. AI only explains.
    2. No clawback from anyone's pay. Taking back pay already earned runs into wage-law trouble. Pay is earned on cash received, so there is nothing to claw back.
    3. No blocking the retailer's next order. Whether the retailer accepts a credit note is between them and finance. Ten Today raises a ticket; it doesn't hold the business hostage.
    4. No GST filing. Nothing is sent to the portal. Finance records what the retailer did on the GST portal, so the tool never claims a status it can't see.

    How it was built, and how it's tested

    Discount controls

    The seven controls, their risk and control matrix, and the rules with effective dates live in the app, each one testable: open the controls · download the matrix and the weekly control MIS from "For finance, tax and audit". 66 tests cover the rules (35 engine, 31 controls).

    Approval desk: what gets done first, who is allowed to say yes, and how you prove it

    The order the list is picked in (4 rules)
    1. A call booked for a fixed time comes first, at the retailer's time
    2. How late it is: 5+ days late → 1–4 days late → due today
    3. Is money waiting on it: a quote sent or a price being negotiated
    4. Days overdue, then deal size, only to break a tie
    The stages a lead moves through

    New lead (1 day) → Qualified (quote) → Quote sent (3 days) → Negotiating → Order placed.
    When a stage moves, the old task closes itself and the next step appears once. Never twice.

    The link to Every Rupee, both ways

    Every approved discount carries a fingerprint (contractSeal, SHA-256) across to Every Rupee. If a retailer later pays short without that approval, the difference comes back as a task for the rep, and it blocks new discount asks until it is settled.

    The rules the controls follow
    • Post-sale discounts, prior agreement removed from 1 Nov 2026 · Finance Act 2026, Notification 03/2026-CT (TaxGuru)
    • s.34(2) deadline: 30 Nov or the annual return date, whichever is earlier (CA Sahuja)
    • IMS and credit notes: accept, reject, pending for one tax period (ClearTax) · what a rejection does to the supplier (Taxilla)
    • Financial credit notes and promotion services: CBIC Circular 251/08/2025 (Business Standard)
    • What's still unsettled: proof of reversal, classification, audit evidence (Nexdigm)
    • 72% of US trade promotions lose money, citing McKinsey (Tellius) · HUL FY25 gross margin 51.15%, used as an illustrative margin

    The domain here is post-sale discounts. The method is not: find the handoff, name the owner, put a clock on it. The same three moves apply to an insurance claim a payer refuses, a payment a customer disputes, or a service credit a client won't sign off.
