Product case study · B2B sales operations · Rupak Ghanpathi, ex-HDFC Bank

100 open leads. 10 actions today.

A mobile To-Do module that tells a salesperson what to act on now, why, and what comes next, without becoming another CRM or a tool for watching people.

Built for dealer and distributor sales in Indian B2B (FMCG, building materials, pharma), where every deal runs through schemes, discounts and dealer credit.

Informed by the ownership buckets, approval queues and ageing MIS I worked with at HDFC Bank.

What this proves: the loop a PM actually runs — vague complaint → written rule → test → screen. Here it's a discount that sits undecided: who owns it, when it escalates, and why the record can't be quietly edited. 27 engine tests, 18 UAT cases, one running product.

How it ships

The demo simulates sign-in so you can try every seat in two minutes; its rules run in your browser. In production the same rules move to the server:

How it's proven: the rules are one module, and it ships with a test suite — 27 engine tests (node --test tests/engine.test.mjs) covering ranking, escalation, credit overrides, the sealed record and the finance handoff, plus 18 UAT cases you can run in the browser. A rule that isn't tested isn't claimed.

  • Sign-in: company accounts (Google or Microsoft) with each person's role and buckets stored on the server. No view switching.
  • Who sees what: the database itself returns only a rep's own tasks (row-level security), even if the page is tampered with.
  • Decisions: approvals, transfers and orders run as server functions, so "a different manager must approve" can't be skipped from the browser.
  • The sealed record: written only by the server and copied to storage the app can't edit.

The companion product, Every Rupee, already runs on real sign-in for its private workspace.

Three problems the product handles

Cherry-picking

Reps call the easy dealers first. The ranking puts what's late and what's blocking money on top, and moving a task doesn't reset its age.

Discounts stuck with the approver

An ask freezes the rep's task and goes to the person allowed to decide it. If it isn't decided within a day, it escalates to the sales head. Dealers over their credit limit or credit days need a written override.

Scheme leakage

A live slab scheme applies without an ask. Creating one needs a second person (maker-checker), which is the control KPMG lists as missing where schemes leak.

Connected to Every Rupee, in both directions

The two are separate products, so they exchange a small versioned record (contract v1) in the link, and each side rejects anything malformed. An approved discount goes to finance with Send to finance, and Every Rupee checks the customer's short payment against that cap. A short payment with no approval comes back to the rep's Today as a recovery task. There's no server and no live sync: one record per link.

Problem, trigger and proposed design

Why: the problem

On a phone, a list of everything is a list of nothing. The quote going cold looks exactly like the 40 records around it.

When: the trigger

A workflow event: a new lead, a quote sent, an approval needed, a callback agreed.
Todayit joins 100+ open records.
Proposedit becomes a task with a target and a reason.

How: the design
  1. Tasks come from workflow events
  2. Tasks close when the stage moves
  3. Managers see exceptions, not activity
The ranking rule · applied in this order
  1. Priority tier: P1 = 5+ days past target · P2 = 1–4 days · P3 = due today
  2. Blocking stage: within a tier, approvals first
  3. Days overdue: older before newer
  4. Deal value: only a tie-break, so a big deal doesn't jump the queue

Why a cap works: the 1-3-5 method Todoist teaches limits a day to nine tasks because long, open-ended lists cause overwhelm and constant triage (Todoist). Ten Today applies the same idea to sales work, with the order set by rules.

Callbacks are pinned at the time the customer agreed. Future tasks wait in an Inbox. Rules, not AI, in V1: less clever, but a salesperson can see why a task is on top.

If the stage moves first, the old task auto-closes and the next one is created. From HDFC: completing an action and completing the business process aren't the same thing.

What ships first, and what waits

V1: Today · Inbox · Overdue · Approvals · On hold with reason and revisit date · auto and manual tasks · complete, reschedule, reassign · four push triggers · manager workload view · history.

Later: AI prioritisation, recurring tasks, outcome-based completion, productivity scoring (it would turn a work tool into performance management).

How I'd know it worked · one metric and a guardrail

% of tasks completed within their original target, measured against the original date so rescheduling can't fake "on time". Guardrail: tasks rescheduled 3+ times or on hold past their revisit date. Why it matters: when Todoist launched Smart Schedule, it said a significant portion of its users reschedule up to 70 tasks day after day (MacStories). Rescheduling is how lists lie, so Ten Today measures against the original target and asks for a blocker on the second reschedule. Revenue is a downstream outcome, not the result.

Validate first: shadow three salespeople for a day, review a month of CRM history, test the Today screen as a clickable prototype with five reps.
